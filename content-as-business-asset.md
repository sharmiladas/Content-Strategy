## The Problem

Content teams are often treated as a cost center — a support function that produces deliverables on request, evaluated by output volume rather than business impact. At a global professional networking platform, I inherited a content organization that needed both: a stronger operating model, and a stronger case for why content strategy deserved a seat in bigger conversations.

## Building the Team and the Governance Model

As Senior Manager, Knowledge Management & Content Strategy, I recruited, trained, and led a 30-person global content strategy team — content strategists, content engineers, and localization engineers — supporting content across 23 languages for 1.5M+ customers and 2M+ weekly sessions.

To bring order to a fast-moving, multi-product content organization, I:

- Launched a content governance steering committee and led the first content inventories and audits to close content gaps across the platform's Help Center
- Drove information architecture and taxonomy strategy for content personalization, and standardized voice, tone, and quality across the customer journey
- Partnered with Engineering to rebuild content infrastructure on a headless CMS and implement a single-sourcing methodology — versioned content delivery across web, mobile, chat, and email
- Administered a metrics program — Google Analytics, Tableau, and quantitative and qualitative analysis including surveys and A/B testing — to monitor content health, adoption, and self-service effectiveness across global audiences

## Making the Business Case: "Content as a Business Asset"

Governance and infrastructure solve the operational problem. The harder problem was reframing how leadership thought about content itself. I introduced **"Content as a Business Asset"** — a framework mapping content investment directly to the customer journey, so content decisions could be evaluated the way any other business investment would be: by the outcomes they drove, not just the output they produced.

I've since presented this framework at industry conferences.

## Impact

- 15% reduction in support cases
- 27% reduction in vendor costs
- 10% improvement in customer satisfaction (CSAT)
- 17% increase in weekly sessions
- Release-synchronized content strategy and single-sourcing across a 30-person global team, 23 languages, 1.5M+ customers

Headless CMS, Google Analytics, Tableau, A/B testing, content governance frameworks
